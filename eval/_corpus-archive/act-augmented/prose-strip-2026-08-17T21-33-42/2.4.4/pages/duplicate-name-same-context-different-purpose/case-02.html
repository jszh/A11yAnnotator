Note on accessibility (why automated tools cannot judge this page): Both links have the identical accessible name "download the form" and sit in the same table cell, so their programmatically determined link context is identical. Each has a valid, complete download href, so automated link-name checks pass. But the two destinations are not equivalent: the first downloads a parental consent form (permission to teach and give first aid), the second a medical history form (allergies and conditions) — genuinely different documents serving genuinely different purposes. The disambiguating words ("that gives us permission…" / "that lists any allergies…") trail each link rather than being part of its name, so a screen-reader user listing links hears "download the form / download the form" and cannot tell which form is which. Deciding that a consent form and a medical-history form are different purposes — not two copies of one document — is a human semantic judgment; no static checker opens the two files, reads them, and compares their purpose.

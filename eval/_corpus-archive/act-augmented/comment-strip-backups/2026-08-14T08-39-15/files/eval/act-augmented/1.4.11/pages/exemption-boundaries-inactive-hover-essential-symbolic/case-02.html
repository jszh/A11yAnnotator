Why automated tools miss this — and why this PASSES: Hover your pointer over a preference row and a faint grey wash (#ededed, ~1.17:1 against white) appears behind it. A contrast scanner that samples the :hover state will compute that ~1.17:1 wash against the page and report a 1.4.11 failure. But the wash is a supplemental hover treatment: the checkbox is already identified at rest by its 2px black border (18.8:1), and the pointer's own position is the indicator of the hover state. Per the SC, such author-supplied hover treatments need not reach 3:1. The black border even stays ~16:1 against the grey wash, so the control never loses identifying contrast. Recognizing "this faint thing is a non-required hover decoration, not a state indicator" is a category judgement a pixel sampler cannot make.

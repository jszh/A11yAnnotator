Coastal Watershed Institute · Field Research Brief

Effects of Riparian Buffer Width on Stream Temperature in the Cedar Basin

A two-season field study of 18 monitored stream reaches, summer 2023–2024.

Authors
M. Ferreira, J. Oduya, R. Lindqvist
Study period
June 2023 – August 2024
Sites monitored
18 stream reaches
Status
Final — peer reviewed

Executive summary

Wider forested riparian buffers were associated with significantly lower midday stream temperatures during peak summer. Reaches with buffers of at least 30 metres averaged 3.2 °C cooler than reaches with buffers under 10 metres, with downstream cooling persisting for several hundred metres.

Key findings

  • Buffer width explained 61% of the variance in maximum daily temperature across sites.
  • The cooling benefit plateaued beyond roughly 35 metres of buffer width.
  • Two reaches with recently thinned canopy showed temperature rebounds within one season.

Recommendation

Maintain a minimum 30-metre forested buffer on temperature-sensitive reaches, and prioritise canopy retention over replanting where mature trees remain.

Methods (abridged)

Continuous temperature loggers recorded at 15-minute intervals at the head and tail of each reach. Buffer width was measured from orthophotos and verified in the field. Full methods and data tables appear in the supplementary appendix.

Special Exhibition

Hokusai: Beyond the Great Wave

February 14 – June 22, 2025 · West Wing, Galleries 7–10

More than 200 woodblock prints, paintings, and illustrated books trace the seven-decade career of Katsushika Hokusai (1760–1849) — far beyond the single image that made him famous. Drawn from the Pelham's holdings and major international loans, the exhibition reframes The Great Wave as one experiment among thousands by a restless, lifelong student of nature.

Organized in five sections, the show opens with Hokusai's early commercial work for Edo publishers and follows his relentless reinvention through the Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, the sprawling Manga sketchbooks, and the luminous brush paintings of his final decade, when he signed himself "the old man mad about drawing."

What you'll see

Highlights include a rare complete set of the Thirty-six Views, several preparatory drawings shown publicly for the first time, and a gallery devoted to Hokusai's influence on European artists from Monet to Van Gogh. A study room lets visitors page through digitized volumes of the Manga at full resolution.

Curator's note

"People know the wave, but they don't know the man who spent sixty years learning to draw water," says guest curator Dr. Naomi Ishikawa. "By the end of the show, the wave looks different — it's the work of someone who never stopped studying."

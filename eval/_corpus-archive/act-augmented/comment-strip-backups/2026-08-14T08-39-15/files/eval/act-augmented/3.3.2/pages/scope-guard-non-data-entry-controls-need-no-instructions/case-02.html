The Quiet Comeback of the Night Train

By Annika Sørensen · 14 min read
Email a link Post to X Share on Facebook Copy link

Across the continent, sleeper services that were written off a decade ago are filling up again. Operators that once mothballed their couchettes now report waiting lists for routes between capitals that, by day, would mean a short flight and a long security queue.

The economics are not obvious. A bed on rails costs more to run than a reclining seat, and the rolling stock is scarce. Yet demand has proven sticky among travellers who would rather sleep through the journey than spend a productive morning at an airport.

Three governments have now folded night routes into their climate targets, subsidising the gap between ticket revenue and the genuine cost of moving a hotel down a track at 160 kilometres an hour.

What follows is a route-by-route look at the revival, and at the quiet engineering problem nobody likes to discuss: where the carriages will come from.

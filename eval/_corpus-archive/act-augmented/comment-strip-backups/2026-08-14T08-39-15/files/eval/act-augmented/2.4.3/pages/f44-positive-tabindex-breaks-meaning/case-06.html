How we cut our CI build time in half

By Priya Raman · 8 min read · March 2025

Our continuous-integration pipeline had crept up to nearly 22 minutes per run, and engineers were context-switching away while they waited. Over a quarter we brought it down to under 11 minutes without buying faster hardware. Here is what actually moved the needle.

1. Cache the dependency graph, not just the modules

The biggest single win came from caching the resolved dependency graph between runs. We were re-resolving the full lockfile on every build even when nothing had changed. You can read the exact configuration in our CI cache guide.

2. Parallelize the test shards by historical timing

Splitting tests evenly by file count left one shard doing twice the work of the others. We now shard by recorded per-test duration, which flattened the slowest shard considerably. The sharding doc has the script.

3. Skip the work that did not change

Affected-target detection means a docs-only change no longer rebuilds the entire app. This required wiring up our monorepo graph, covered in the affected-targets walkthrough.

Put together, these three changes roughly halved our median build. The rollout details — and the one rollback we had to do — are in the rollout notes.

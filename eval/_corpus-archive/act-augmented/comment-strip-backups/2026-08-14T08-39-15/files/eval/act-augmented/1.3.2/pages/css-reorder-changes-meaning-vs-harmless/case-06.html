Currents

Glass from the Eastern Mediterranean, 100 BCE – 400 CE

About the exhibition

The invention of glassblowing on the Levantine coast in the first century BCE turned a luxury into an everyday material almost overnight. Currents traces that shift through eighty vessels recovered from harbours, shipwrecks and household middens between Sidon and Alexandria.

The earliest pieces here are still cast and ground — heavy, opaque, imitating semi-precious stone. Within two generations the blown forms arrive: thin-walled jugs, ribbed bowls, the ubiquitous unguentaria that carried perfume across the whole Roman world.

The closing room follows the trade outward, with a Sidonian beaker found as far north as a grave in the Rhineland — evidence of how quickly the new technique, and the objects it made, travelled the currents of the ancient Mediterranean.

Chapter 3: Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the set of metabolic reactions that convert the chemical energy stored in nutrients into adenosine triphosphate (ATP). In eukaryotes the process unfolds across three connected stages, each treated in its own section below. This chapter assumes you have read Chapter 2 on membrane transport.

3.1 Glycolysis

Glycolysis takes place in the cytosol and splits one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, producing a small net yield of ATP and NADH. It requires no oxygen and is shared by nearly all living cells, which is why it is treated first.

The pathway is conventionally divided into an energy-investment phase and an energy-payoff phase; the net reaction yields two ATP and two NADH per glucose.

3.2 The Citric Acid Cycle

Once pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix it is oxidised to acetyl-CoA, which feeds the citric acid cycle (also called the Krebs cycle). Each turn of the cycle releases carbon dioxide and reduces the electron carriers NAD+ and FAD. Because two pyruvate molecules arise from each glucose, the cycle turns twice per glucose molecule.

The point students most often misread is what happens to the carriers reduced here. They do not themselves produce most of the cell's ATP; instead they hand their electrons to the next stage. That handoff is important enough to warrant its own discussion immediately below.

3.2.1 Fate of the Reduced Carriers

The NADH and FADH2 generated during the citric acid cycle (and, to a lesser extent, during glycolysis) are not energy currency the cell can spend directly. Each reduced carrier shuttles a pair of high-energy electrons to the inner mitochondrial membrane. There the electrons are passed down a series of protein complexes — the subject of the next section — while the carriers themselves are re-oxidised and returned to the cycle. In other words, this paragraph closes the loop on the very molecules the citric acid cycle produces; it makes no sense except as a continuation of section 3.2.

3.3 Oxidative Phosphorylation

In the final stage, the electron transport chain uses the electrons delivered by NADH and FADH2 to pump protons across the inner membrane, and ATP synthase harnesses the resulting gradient to phosphorylate ADP. This stage accounts for the large majority of the ATP produced from a molecule of glucose, completing the chapter.

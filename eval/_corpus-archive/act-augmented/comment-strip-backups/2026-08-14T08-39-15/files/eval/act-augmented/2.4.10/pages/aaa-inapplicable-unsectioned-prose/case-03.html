Chief Joseph’s Surrender Speech

Spoken by Chief Joseph (Hin-mah-too-yah-lat-kekt) of the Nez Perce, October 5, 1877, as recorded at the surrender in the Bear Paw Mountains, Montana Territory. Public domain.

Tell General Howard I know his heart. What he told me before, I have it in my heart. I am tired of fighting. Our chiefs are killed; Looking Glass is dead, Too-hul-hul-sote is dead. The old men are all dead. It is the young men who say yes or no.

He who led on the young men is dead. It is cold, and we have no blankets; the little children are freezing to death. My people, some of them, have run away to the hills, and have no blankets, no food. No one knows where they are — perhaps freezing to death. I want to have time to look for my children, and see how many of them I can find. Maybe I shall find them among the dead.

Hear me, my chiefs! I am tired; my heart is sick and sad. From where the sun now stands I will fight no more forever.

About this text

The words above are reproduced verbatim from the most widely transmitted English-language record of the speech, as reported by Lieutenant Charles Erskine Scott Wood, who was present at the surrender. The speech was delivered orally; no written sections, headings, or divisions existed in the original utterance, and none have been added here. The library’s policy is to present historical oratory as a single continuous text exactly as transmitted, adding only this curatorial note and the bibliographic byline above. Scholars dispute the precise wording of the final lines; this edition follows the Wood transcription. The text is in the public domain.

Apply for a residential parking permit

Residents in a controlled parking zone (CPZ) can apply for an annual permit to park on the street near their home. Read who qualifies, what to bring, and the fees before you start.

Who can apply

You can apply if your home is inside a controlled parking zone and you are the registered keeper of the vehicle, or you have the keeper's written permission to use it as your main vehicle.

  • The vehicle must be taxed, insured and roadworthy.
  • You may hold a permit for up to two vehicles per household.
  • Company vehicles qualify only if the company address matches your home.

What you'll need

Have these ready before you begin; you can upload photos or scans during the application.

  • Proof of address dated within the last three months (utility bill, council tax, or tenancy agreement).
  • The vehicle registration document (V5C) or a leasing certificate.
  • A valid debit or credit card for the fee.

Fees

Fees depend on the vehicle's emissions band and are charged annually. Permits renew on the anniversary of issue.

How to appeal a refusal

If your application is refused you will get a decision letter explaining why. You have the right to ask the council to look at the decision again.

  • You must submit your appeal within 21 days of the date on the decision letter.
  • Include your application reference, the reason you believe the refusal was wrong, and any document the original application was missing.
  • If your appeal relies on a Blue Badge, medical need, or carer arrangement, attach the supporting evidence — these are the most common grounds for a successful review.
A different officer from the one who made the original decision reviews every appeal. You will normally get a written outcome within 15 working days. If the review upholds the refusal, the letter will explain how to escalate to the independent parking adjudicator, which is the final stage.

There is no fee to appeal. Submitting an appeal does not extend any existing permit, so if your current permit is about to expire you should still renew it while the appeal is being considered.

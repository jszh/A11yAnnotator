Getting Started with Lumen Thermostat

Updated 2 weeks ago · 6 min read

Welcome! Let's get your Lumen up and running. Start by switching off power to your heating system at the breaker — this matters, so don't skip it — then gently pull your old thermostat off its wall plate and take a photo of the wiring before you disconnect anything. Lumen uses the same R, W, Y, G, and C terminals most North American systems use, and the included label stickers help you tag each wire as you remove it. Snap the Lumen base plate to the wall, feed the labeled wires into the matching terminals, and click the display unit onto the base until you feel it seat. Turn the breaker back on; the screen should glow white.

With the unit powered, the next thing to do is get it onto your home network. On the thermostat screen, tap the gear icon, choose Network, and pick your Wi-Fi name from the list. Lumen only supports 2.4 GHz networks, so if you don't see your network, make sure you're not looking at the 5 GHz band — many routers broadcast both with similar names. Enter your password using the on-screen keypad. Once it connects, the cloud icon in the top corner turns solid, and you can finish setup from the Lumen phone app, which is where you'll do everything else.

Now for the part that actually saves you money: a heating schedule. In the app, open the calendar tab and tap any day to add a temperature setpoint and the time it should take effect. A typical weekday has four points — a warm wake-up, a cooler away-from-home daytime, a comfortable evening, and a cool overnight — and you can copy a day you like onto every other day with the "apply to all weekdays" button. Setpoints can be as granular as fifteen-minute increments. The app shows a little energy-leaf estimate as you build the schedule, so you can see roughly what each change costs or saves before you commit it.

If your routine isn't regular, geofencing may suit you better than a fixed schedule. When you enable Location in the app and grant the permission your phone asks for, Lumen draws an invisible boundary around your home; when the last household phone leaves that boundary, the thermostat eases back to your away setpoint, and when the first phone returns, it starts warming the house so it's comfortable by the time you walk in. You can set the radius of the boundary and decide whether geofencing overrides the schedule or just fills in the gaps. Everyone who should count toward "someone's home" needs the app installed and Location granted on their own phone.

Finally, if your Lumen won't come online, work through these in order. First confirm the screen has power at all — a dark screen usually means a tripped breaker or a wire that backed out of its terminal, so recheck the photo you took. If the screen is on but the cloud icon stays hollow, you're almost always on the wrong band; forget the network on the thermostat and re-add the 2.4 GHz one specifically. If it joins the network but the app still says "offline," sign out of the app and back in to refresh the cloud link, and if that fails, hold the gear icon for ten seconds to restart the unit. Still stuck? Contact support with the serial number from the back of the display unit.

Café owners protest new permit fees outside city hall

Dozens of independent coffee shops say a steep rise in sidewalk-seating permits could force them to remove outdoor tables before summer.

By Marcus Hale · Local Affairs Correspondent · Published June 18

More than forty small-business owners gathered on the steps of city hall on Tuesday morning to protest a proposed sixfold increase in the annual fee for sidewalk-seating permits. The fee, currently set at 180 dollars, would climb to just over 1,100 dollars under a budget amendment the council is expected to vote on next week.

"Outdoor seating is not a luxury for us — it's half our summer revenue," said Dana Whitfield, who has run a corner café on Maple Street for eleven years. "This fee would wipe out the margin entirely."

City officials defend the increase as a way to recover the cost of inspecting and maintaining public walkways. A spokesperson for the public-works department noted that the fee had not been adjusted in more than a decade and that neighboring towns charge comparable amounts.

Several council members have signaled they may seek a compromise — perhaps a tiered fee scaled to the number of tables, or a phase-in over three years rather than a single jump.

The business owners have launched a petition that, organizers say, has already gathered more than two thousand signatures. They plan to address the council directly during the public-comment period before next week's vote.

Galleries of Early Britain

A walk through eight centuries, from the legions to the longships. Each gallery below gathers the objects that define its age.

c. AD 43 – 410

Roman Britain

Four centuries of occupation left Britain criss-crossed with roads, walled towns and villas. This gallery centres on everyday life in the province of Britannia — the soldier, the merchant and the household shrine.

Samian-ware bowl, terra sigillata
Red-gloss tableware imported from Gaulish kilns, found at Verulamium.
Legionary pay chit (stipendium tablet)
A wax tablet recording a soldier's deduction for boots and bedding.
Altar to Mithras
Dedicated by a centurion of the Sixth Legion; carved tufa, 3rd century.

c. AD 410 – 793

Anglo-Saxon Treasures

After Rome withdrew, new kingdoms rose across the island. Gold, garnet and the great ship-burials speak of warrior aristocracies and a craft tradition of dazzling skill.

Cloisonné shoulder-clasp
Gold inlaid with garnet and millefiori glass, in the Sutton Hoo manner.
Pattern-welded seax
A single-edged knife with an inscribed blade and bone handle.

c. AD 793 – 1066

The Viking Incursions

Raiding gave way to settlement as Scandinavian fleets wintered on English soil. The objects here trace trade, silver hoards and the slow merging of two cultures.

Hacksilver hoard
Cut arm-rings and coin fragments weighed for bullion exchange.
Trefoil brooch
Cast copper-alloy dress fitting, Borre-style interlace.

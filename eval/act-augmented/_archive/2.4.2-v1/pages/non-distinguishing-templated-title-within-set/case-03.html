Chapter 3: Cellular Respiration

How cells release the energy stored in glucose to make ATP.

In Chapter 2 you learned the structure of the mitochondrion. In this chapter we trace a single glucose molecule through glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation, accounting for every ATP produced along the way.

3.1 An Overview of Energy Harvest

Cellular respiration is the controlled, stepwise oxidation of glucose. Rather than burning it in one violent reaction, the cell strips electrons a little at a time and banks the energy in a proton gradient.

3.2 Glycolysis

Glycolysis happens in the cytosol and splits one six-carbon glucose into two three-carbon pyruvate molecules, netting two ATP and two NADH. It is ancient and oxygen-independent, which is why it is shared by nearly every living thing.

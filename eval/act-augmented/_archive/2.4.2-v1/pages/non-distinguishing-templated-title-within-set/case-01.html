Why we migrated our ingest pipeline to Rust

Posted March 2, 2024  ·  by Dana Okonkwo, Staff Engineer  ·  8 min read
rustdata-pipelineperformance

For the last three years our event-ingest service was written in Python. It was easy to evolve, but by late 2023 the p99 latency under the holiday traffic spike had crept past 400 ms and the garbage-collector pauses were showing up directly in our SLO dashboards. This post walks through why we rewrote the hot path in Rust, what we measured, and the two bugs the borrow checker caught before they ever reached staging.

The first surprise was memory. The Python workers held roughly 1.9 GB resident per pod under load; the Rust replacement settled at 240 MB and never moved. That alone let us cut the ingest fleet from 48 pods to 11.

We rolled this out region by region across February, and on March 1 we flipped the last region. The graph the next morning is the reason this post exists: the latency band that used to oscillate was, for the first time, a flat line. The rest of this article covers the migration playbook so other teams at NimbusData can repeat it.

Lorem Ipsum: The History and Use of Placeholder Text

Open almost any design template and you will meet a familiar block of pseudo-Latin beginning "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet." It is the most widely used filler text in publishing and web design — but where did it come from, and why do designers still reach for it?

Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit, sed do eiusmod tempor incididunt ut labore et dolore magna aliqua.

A scrambled passage of Cicero

Despite looking like gibberish, Lorem ipsum is derived from a first-century-BC text by the Roman statesman Cicero, De finibus bonorum et malorum ("On the Ends of Good and Evil"). The familiar opening is a garbled fragment of a passage about the pursuit of pleasure, with words clipped and rearranged into nonsense.

Why designers use nonsense on purpose

The point of placeholder text is to let a reader judge the shape of a layout — line length, rhythm, and density — without being distracted by the meaning of real copy. Because Lorem ipsum has a roughly normal distribution of word lengths, it mimics the visual texture of English prose far better than repeating text text text.

When to remove it

The cardinal rule is simple: placeholder text must never ship to production. Filler left in a published page — including in the <title> element — is a classic accessibility and quality failure, because it tells readers nothing about the actual content. This article is about that very pitfall, which is why its own title names the subject precisely.

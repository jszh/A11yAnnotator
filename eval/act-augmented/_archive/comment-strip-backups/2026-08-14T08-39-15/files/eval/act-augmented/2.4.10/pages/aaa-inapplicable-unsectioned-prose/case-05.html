IN THE SUPERIOR COURT OF THE COUNTY OF ALDERWICK
Helena R. Bray, Plaintiff, v. Coastline Freight Ltd., Defendant — Case No. 2025-CV-0413

Witness Statement of Margaret A. Fenwick

  1. I, Margaret Anne Fenwick, of 18 Harbour Lane, Alderwick, am the witness in this matter and make this statement from my own knowledge except where I indicate that it is from information and belief, in which case I state the source.
  2. On the evening of 6 November 2025 I left my shift at the Harbour Lane bakery shortly after closing, which is at half past six, and began walking south along the eastern footway of Marsh Road toward the bus stop opposite the chandlery.
  3. The light had gone but the street lamps were lit and working, and the road surface was wet from rain that had stopped perhaps twenty minutes before. I was not in any hurry and I was paying ordinary attention to my surroundings.
  4. As I drew level with the entrance to the freight yard I heard, before I saw anything, the particular note of a heavy diesel engine being worked hard, and I looked up toward the yard gates to my right.
  5. A large flatbed lorry, which I now understand belonged to the defendant, was reversing out of the yard and across the footway and into Marsh Road. It did not stop at the footway line, and I did not hear any reversing alarm, which I would have expected to hear and which I have heard from other lorries at that gate on other days.
  6. Ms. Bray was on the footway a little ahead of me, perhaps four or five paces, walking in the same direction I was. I do not believe she could have seen the lorry until it was already across the footway, because the yard wall blocks the view of the gate until you are nearly upon it.
  7. I called out as loudly as I could. Ms. Bray turned, stepped back toward the wall, and the rear corner of the flatbed caught her right shoulder and spun her against the railings. The lorry stopped only after a man ran out of the yard waving both arms.
  8. I went to Ms. Bray at once. She was conscious and speaking but in evident pain and unable to lift her right arm. I stayed with her until the ambulance arrived, which I estimate was about fifteen minutes later, and I gave my name and number to the police officer who attended.
  9. I have read this statement, it is true to the best of my knowledge and belief, and I make it knowing that if it is tendered in evidence I may be liable to prosecution if I have wilfully stated anything I know to be false or do not believe to be true.

Sworn at Alderwick this 9th day of January 2026, before me, a Commissioner for Oaths.

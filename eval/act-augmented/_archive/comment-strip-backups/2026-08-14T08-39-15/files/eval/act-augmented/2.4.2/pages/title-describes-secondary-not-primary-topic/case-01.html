Council approves $214M Cedar Falls budget after marathon vote on police and parks funding

By Marisol Ortega, City Hall reporter · Updated 6:42 a.m. CT

After nearly six hours of public comment and a flurry of last-minute amendments, the Cedar Falls City Council voted 5–2 early Wednesday to adopt a $214 million operating budget for the coming fiscal year, locking in a contested 3.1% property-tax levy increase and reshaping how the city funds public safety and parks.

The largest single line item — $61.4 million for the police department — drew the sharpest debate. Council members Dana Whitfield and Léo Brassard pushed an amendment to redirect $2.3 million toward a new civilian crisis-response team; it failed 4–3 after the police union warned the shift would delay the hiring of nine patrol officers already budgeted last year.

"This budget reflects hard tradeoffs, not a wish list," said Mayor Priya Venkataraman, who broke from two of her usual allies to support the final package. "We are funding the basics — roads, water, public safety — while finally putting real money into the parks that residents have asked us about at every single town hall."

Parks and recreation will see its capital allocation rise 18% to $9.8 million, funding the long-delayed renovation of Riverside Park's flood-damaged trail network and a new aquatic center on the city's east side. The increase was the central demand of a coalition of neighborhood associations that packed Tuesday's chamber.

To close a projected $7.6 million shortfall, the council leaned on the levy increase and a one-time draw from the city's rainy-day reserve, which finance director Aaron Kuhl said would leave the fund at 14% of annual expenditures — just above the 12% floor the council adopted in 2022.

The two dissenting votes came from members representing the city's north-side wards, who argued the property-tax increase would fall hardest on fixed-income homeowners. "We are balancing this budget on the backs of seniors who already can't keep up," said Councilmember Tomás Reyes, who proposed a smaller 1.9% levy that failed for lack of a second.

The adopted budget takes effect July 1. A detailed line-item breakdown and the full amendment log will be posted to the city clerk's portal within ten business days, officials said.

Newton’s Laws of Motion

Part I: Mechanics · Chapter 3 of 41

Having established kinematics in Chapter 2, we now turn to the causes of motion. This chapter introduces the three laws Sir Isaac Newton set out in the Principia (1687) and shows how they unify the descriptions of rest, uniform motion, and acceleration under a single framework of forces.

3.1  The First Law: Inertia

A body remains at rest, or in uniform motion in a straight line, unless acted upon by a net external force. The tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of motion is called inertia, and the quantitative measure of inertia is the object’s mass.

3.2  The Second Law: F = ma

The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass:

ΣF = m·a

3.3  The Third Law: Action and Reaction

For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. When body A exerts a force on body B, body B simultaneously exerts a force of equal magnitude and opposite direction on body A.

Why the £14m harbour dredge was paused three weeks before the season

By Marian Okafor · Updated 6 June 2026 · 9 min read

The dredging contract that was meant to deepen the eastern channel before the summer fishing season has been suspended, and the harbour authority will not say for how long. Documents obtained by The Tidemark Review show the pause was ordered after sediment samples taken in April exceeded the disposal licence's threshold for two metals.

The authority's chief engineer told councillors in a closed session that resuming work would require a fresh marine licence variation — a process that, on past form, takes between four and seven months. None of that timeline has been shared with the fishing co-operative whose vessels depend on the deeper channel.

Our reporting on this contract began eighteen months ago, and the thread connecting the suspended dredge to the 2024 disposal-site dispute, the contractor's earlier overruns, and the council's own environmental sub-committee is not obvious from any single story. The follow-up pieces listed alongside this article trace that thread in order.

What is clear is that the season will open with the channel at its current depth. Skippers we spoke to said the larger boats will again have to wait for high water to enter and leave — adding hours to every trip and, several said, making some grounds uneconomic to fish at all.

The contractor declined to comment beyond confirming that work is "temporarily demobilised at the authority's instruction." The authority did not respond to detailed questions before publication.

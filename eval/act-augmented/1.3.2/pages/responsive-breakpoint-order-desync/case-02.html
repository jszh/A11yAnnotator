The Riverside Contract

By Priya Raman · 18 min read · April 2, 2026

For eleven months the city insisted the waterfront cleanup contract had gone to the lowest qualified bidder. The paperwork said otherwise, but the paperwork was sealed.

A bid that wasn't a bid

The first thread came loose in a parking-permit database — an oddity that, pulled slowly, unspooled into a procurement file no one was supposed to see. The numbers did not add up, and the names attached to them were familiar.

We spent four months confirming what a single anonymous packet had alleged, because the packet alone proved nothing. The reader deserves to watch us earn the conclusion, not be handed it.

The meeting that never appeared on a calendar

Two officials maintained they had never met the contractor. A garage timestamp and a catering invoice, reconstructed here for the first time, say they shared a table for ninety minutes on a Tuesday in October.

What follows is the full account of how the contract was steered, and who finally decided to talk.

Editor's note

As detailed above, the source who provided the sealed procurement file was the contractor's own former finance director, Dana Whitcomb — and the investigation ended with the contract rescinded and two officials indicted. We name Whitcomb here, at the close, only because the body of the piece establishes the corroboration first.

The tidal marsh is coming back

By R. Okafor · Coastwatch Dispatch · Field notes, October

For thirty years the Halden estuary was diked off for grazing land, and the salt marsh behind it slowly suffocated. In 2019 the county breached the old dike in three places and let the tide return on its own schedule.

The recovery has outpaced every projection. Ribbed mussels recolonized within a season; cordgrass followed; this autumn the first juvenile flounder were netted in the restored channels. “Five winters ago this channel was a parking lot of dead reeds,” said the lead ecologist. “Now the ribbed mud breathes again.”

What surprised the team most was the speed of the sediment rebound. The marsh is accreting nearly a centimetre of new mud a year — fast enough, the models suggest, to keep pace with the projected rise in sea level through mid-century.

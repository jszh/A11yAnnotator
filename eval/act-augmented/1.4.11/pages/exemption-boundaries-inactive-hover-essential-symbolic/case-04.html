Same controls, accessible contrast (reference)

Why automated tools miss this: The close and navigation controls are literal text characters — &#10005; (✕), &#8249; (‹), &#8250; (›) — used purely for their shape, not as language. The SC classifies such symbolic characters as non-text content that must reach 3:1. Many contrast tools treat these as ordinary TEXT and either evaluate them under the 4.5:1 text rule, or skip them because they are 1-character "icons," or — having an accessible aria-label — assume the control is fine. The failing glyphs sit at ~1.4:1 (#5e6b82 on #4a5366); a sighted low-vision user cannot find Close or Next. Recognizing that an X here is a graphic object, not a letter, is a semantic call no scanner makes reliably.

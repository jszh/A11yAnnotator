# SC 2.4.10 Section Headings — augmented test corpus

The ACT-rules / shipping-checker coverage of SC 2.4.10 (Section Headings, Level AAA) is thin and proxy-shaped: automated tooling can confirm that heading *elements* exist, are programmatically exposed, and are not skipped in level, but it cannot evaluate the criterion's actual normative obligation — that *each section of a web page, when the page is organized into sections, is introduced by a heading whose text names that section*. The detectable surface collapses 2.4.10 into a binary "the document has at least one heading" check and treats any non-empty `role=heading` as satisfying it. This corpus augments that gap with human-judgment pages that probe the per-section obligation: a genuine mid-document section left unheaded while its siblings are headed; visually-demarcated sections (cards, columns, bands, rules, gaps) with no programmatic heading per section; headings whose *text* is wrong, copy-pasted, placeholder, or generic and therefore does not introduce its own section; multiple distinct navigation/landmark regions with no demarcating heading (the H69 nav sub-limb); heading *levels* that contradict the true topical containment hierarchy; present-but-nameless headings (whitespace-only, decorative/CSS-image-only, dingbat or PUA glyph) that expose a heading node carrying no section name; and the AAA applicability gate itself — genuinely unsectioned prose (a letter, a transcribed oration, a continuous narrative) that correctly does **not** require headings, guarding against the naive "long text + few headings = fail" false positive.

All seven aspects are finalized with 6 valid human-judgment pages each (5 fail/applicability cases plus an intentional pass/boundary control per aspect); none is short of the 5-valid-page bar. Every page is marked `requiresHumanJudgment: true` and `recommendation: keep`. Reviewer notes captured during scoring (kept verbatim in `summary.json`) flag several non-blocking issues that do not change any verdict: a handful of prompt/metadata-vs-file selector mismatches in the `nameless-heading-element` aspect (cases 01–03 — the on-disk artifacts were judged authoritative), undefined `sr-only` / `visually-hidden` helper classes on unrelated table captions, a copy-paste narrative artifact in `semantic-heading-level-mismatch` case-04's `.md`, and the expected AAA-interpretation caveats on the `multiple-navigation-regions-unheaded` and `semantic-heading-level-mismatch` aspects, where the FAIL reading leans on the H69/G141 advisory sub-limbs rather than the SC's bright-line normative text — precisely the contextual judgment this corpus is built to exercise.

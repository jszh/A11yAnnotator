Building My First Mechanical Keyboard

by Sam · posted 8 June 2026 · solderingQMKhot-swap

I finally built a 65% keyboard from a kit this weekend. It took about four hours and one minor burn. Here's the whole process so you can avoid the mistakes I made — especially around firmware, which nearly defeated me.

Why I built one

I'd been typing on a mushy membrane board for years. I wanted tactile switches, a layout I could remap, and something I could repair myself instead of binning. A barebones kit plus switches and keycaps came to about $140 — cheaper than most pre-built boards and far more satisfying.

More

Here's exactly what I ordered. Buy the stabilisers separately — the ones bundled with most kits rattle.

  • PCB + case: a hot-swap 65% aluminium kit (gasket-mounted)
  • Switches: 70× Gateron Brown (you need spares)
  • Keycaps: a PBT double-shot Cherry-profile set
  • Stabilisers: screw-in PCB-mount, lubed with dielectric grease
  • Tools: switch puller, keycap puller, hex key (usually included)

Stuff

My kit was hot-swap, so technically no soldering — but the per-key RGB LEDs on the underside still needed it. If your board is solder-only, the same idea applies to every switch.

  1. Seat the LED on the correct polarity (the flat corner faces the silkscreen mark).
  2. Tack one leg, check the LED sits flush, then solder the remaining legs.
  3. Trim the legs flush and test continuity before moving on.

Other

This is the part the tutorials gloss over. The board ships with default firmware, but to remap keys you flash your own QMK build. Put the board into bootloader mode first (short the reset pads or hold Esc while plugging in).

qmk setup
qmk compile -kb my65 -km default
qmk flash   -kb my65 -km default

If qmk flash can't find the device, it's almost always that the board never entered bootloader mode — not a driver problem. That cost me an hour.

Was it worth it?

Completely. Typing feels better, I learned to solder, and I can swap a dead switch in thirty seconds. Next time I'll lube the switches before assembly instead of after.

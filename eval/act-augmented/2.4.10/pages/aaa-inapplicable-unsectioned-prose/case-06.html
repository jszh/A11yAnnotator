Field Journal · Day 47

21 March

Approx. 14°S, 142°W · broad reach, 12–15 kn

Woke before the alarm again, that grey not-quite-light when the boat tells you the wind has shifted before your eyes are even open. It had backed maybe twenty degrees in the night and the self-steering was fighting it, so the first thing today, before coffee, before anything, was up on deck in yesterday's clothes to ease the sheet and re-trim. The sea is long and easy this morning, big slow swells from the south that lift the stern and let it down again so gently you could almost sleep through them, which is the opposite of three days ago and I am not going to pretend I'm not grateful.

The windvane has been making a noise I don't like — a faint knock at the bottom of each stroke — and I finally got the courage to hang off the stern with a line round my waist and look at it properly. The lower pintle has worn, there's play in it, and I packed it with a strip cut from an old fender for now. It's a bodge and I know it's a bodge but it's quiet again and quiet is everything out here; the sound of a thing about to break is the loudest sound in the world when you're the only one who can fix it.

I keep thinking about the argument with Dad at the dock. He didn't say don't go — he never says the thing directly — he said "you've always needed to prove something to somebody" and then helped me coil the docklines anyway, which is so completely him that I laughed about it for the first hundred miles and then somewhere around Day 30 it stopped being funny and started being a question I can't put down. Maybe he's right. Maybe out here past everyone's opinion is the only place the question goes quiet too.

Ate well, for once: the last of the real onions fried soft with a tin of beans and a great deal of pepper, eaten straight from the pan wedged in the companionway so it couldn't slide. There are eleven onions left and I am rationing them like they're the last onions on earth, which, for my purposes, they are.

Then this afternoon the dolphins came. A dozen of them, maybe more, riding the bow wave for the better part of an hour, turning on their sides to look up at me with that eye that seems to know something you don't. I lay flat on the foredeck with my chin over the toerail and watched them until they peeled off all at once, the way they always do, as if someone had called them in. I didn't get a single decent photo and I'm glad. Some things you're meant to just keep.

Tonight the doubt is back, the way it always comes at dusk when the light goes and the boat feels smaller. Eleven hundred miles to the Marquesas, give or take, and the water-maker is making a smell I'll have to deal with tomorrow. But the vane is quiet and the onions are holding and the dolphins came, and on a passage you learn to count exactly those kinds of things and let the rest wait until there's light to see it by. To bed. Wind holding from the southeast.

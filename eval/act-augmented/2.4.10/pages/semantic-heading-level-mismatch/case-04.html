Help with Heating & Cooling Costs

Riverside County administers two separate financial-assistance programs for residents struggling with home energy bills. They are funded differently, have different eligibility rules, and you may apply to either or both. This page describes the first program in detail, then introduces the second.

Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP)

LIHEAP is a federally funded benefit that helps eligible households pay for heating and cooling. The county receives an annual block grant and disburses it until funds run out, so applying early in the season is recommended.

Who qualifies for LIHEAP

Households at or below 60% of the state median income qualify. Priority goes to homes with a member who is elderly, disabled, or under age six.

How to apply for LIHEAP

Submit the energy-assistance application with a recent utility bill and proof of income. Processing takes about three weeks.

Start a LIHEAP application

Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP)

The Weatherization Assistance Program is an entirely different benefit. Rather than paying a bill, WAP sends contractors to make your home permanently more energy-efficient — adding insulation, sealing drafts, and repairing or replacing unsafe heating equipment at no cost to you. It is funded by the Department of Energy, not the LIHEAP block grant, and has its own income test and waiting list.

Who qualifies for WAP

Households at or below 200% of the federal poverty level qualify. Receiving LIHEAP automatically makes you income-eligible for WAP, but you must still apply separately.

How to apply for WAP

Complete the weatherization intake form and schedule a home energy audit. Because the work is scheduled by contractor availability, the waiting list can run several months.

Not sure which fits your situation? Call the county energy-help line at (951) 555-0142. The two programs above are independent — one pays a bill, the other fixes the home — so many residents apply to both.

Troubleshooting a stuck rollout

Diagnose why a new deployment never reaches the Running state.

1. Inspect the pods

Step 1. List the pods in the namespace and read the output by column:

$ kubectl get pods -n payments
NAME                       READY   STATUS             RESTARTS   AGE
checkout-7d9f8c6b4-2xk9q   1/1     Running            0          6d
checkout-7d9f8c6b4-pl4mn   0/1     CrashLoopBackOff   8          4m
ledger-5c7b9d4f6-qq8wz     1/1     Running            0          6d
ledger-5c7b9d4f6-zt2vr     0/1     ImagePullBackOff   0          4m
worker-6f4c8a9e2-h7n3d     2/2     Running            1          6d

Step 2. Scan down the STATUS column. Any value other than Running is your culprit. Then cross-reference the same row's READY count (e.g. 0/1) and its RESTARTS total to tell a crash loop apart from an image-pull failure.

In the output above, two rows fail: the second checkout pod is in CrashLoopBackOff with 8 restarts, and the second ledger pod is stuck on ImagePullBackOff. Compare their READY columns against the healthy rows to confirm.

2. Next steps

For a crash loop, pull the previous container's logs; for an image-pull error, verify the registry credentials in the namespace's pull secret.

The Oyster Wars Are Back, and This Time the Water Is Warmer

By Dana Okafor · 21 June 2026

For three generations the Holloway family has worked the same lease off Tilghman Island, dragging for the bivalve that built half the towns on this shore. This spring, for the first time, they hauled up empty cages. The culprit, scientists at the Virginia Institute of Marine Science say, is a warming bay that has pushed a parasitic disease north into waters that were once too cold to sustain it.

The dispute over who may harvest the remaining beds has revived a conflict that historians thought was settled a century ago. State watermen and their counterparts across the line have filed competing petitions; the Potomac River Fisheries Commission meets next month to weigh new catch limits that could shutter dozens of small operations.
